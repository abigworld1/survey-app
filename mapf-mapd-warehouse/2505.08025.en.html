In short: PRISM is a decentralized framework for multi-task multi-agent pathfinding that shares motion constraints through rapidly propagated information packets and performs local constraint-based replanning under limited communication. The paper argues that PRISM is complete for solvable cases and resolves deadlocks when possible, while experiments against CBS and TPTS report strong scalability and solution quality in narrow passages and low-connectivity settings.

What is this paper about?

The paper studies multi-task multi-agent pathfinding, in which several autonomous agents share an environment and must complete individual or assigned tasks without collisions. Agents know the environment but initially do not know the locations, objectives, or paths of other agents, and direct communication is limited by range. Multi-hop communication is possible within a connected component, so the entire team need not form one continuously connected network. The objective is to produce a collision-free team plan while minimizing sum-of-costs, the total cost accumulated by all agents.

What is new compared with prior work?

PRISM contributes a framework that combines decentralized task management, online replanning, and constraint-based pathfinding for MT-MAPF. Its central communication contribution is an information-packet mechanism that carries motion-constraint information beyond the current direct communication neighborhood. These packets are incorporated into local Modified Conflict-Based Search, where relevant information can be treated similarly to virtual agents when resolving conflicts. The paper also presents a theoretical argument that PRISM is complete and can resolve solvable deadlock situations. Finally, it evaluates PRISM in five environments and 25 random scenarios against centralized CBS and decentralized Token Passing with Task Swaps, or TPTS.

What is the key technical idea?

PRISM uses a three-phase planning scheme together with a Modified CBS procedure for planning within local networks. Each agent maintains an identifier, its current task, a planned path, active motion constraints, and a collection of information packets, and it replans when relevant paths, tasks, or communication conditions change. A task allocator assigns valid tasks and may reassign tasks that have not started, while a mission task becomes locked to its agent once execution begins. Information packets communicate constraint adjustments to relevant agents, including agents that later move out of communication range. A regular flushing process removes stale or incorrect information so that replanning does not rely indefinitely on obsolete constraints. Local search then resolves conflicts involving relevant agents and branches over constraint alternatives until a collision-free plan is found or the search proves that no plan exists under the considered conditions.

Detailed section summary

Background

Multi-agent pathfinding coordinates autonomous agents that move through shared spaces while avoiding conflicts and pursuing efficiency objectives. It is relevant to assembly, evacuation, formation, localization, and object transportation, among other applications. Centralized solvers can use global state information to coordinate agents effectively, but their state space and interaction complexity grow rapidly, and they are often less suitable for online adaptation. Decentralized solvers distribute computation and can be more resilient to changing conditions, but they must coordinate using incomplete and local information.

Technical highlights

The key technical device is the information packet, which preserves and disseminates motion constraints when agents leave one another’s communication range. Conclusion

PRISM is presented as a way to adapt constraint-based search to decentralized, online multi-agent planning through selective communication. The theoretical analysis claims completeness and deadlock avoidance for solvable cases under the paper’s assumptions.
