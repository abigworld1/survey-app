In short: This paper studies Collaborative Task Sequencing Multi-Agent Path Finding, in which agents must visit ordered intermediate tasks, avoid collisions, and then reach their destinations. Its CTS-CBS method combines collaborative task-sequence search with constrained path planning, and the reported experiments show large success-rate and runtime gains for its MG-MAPF adaptations, with a limited loss in solution quality.

What is this paper about?

Standard Multi-Agent Path Finding asks multiple agents to move from specified starts to specified goals without collisions in a shared environment. The paper considers a generalization called CTS-MAPF, where agents must also visit intermediate task locations in a prescribed order before reaching their final destinations. The task order and assignment are collaborative because the sequence selected for one agent can affect the planning results of the others. The optimization objective is to minimize an overall movement cost, such as total time or distance, including waiting actions, while satisfying collision, assignment, ordering, and destination requirements.

What is new compared with prior work?

The main contribution is Collaborative Task Sequencing Conflict-Based Search, or CTS-CBS, a method designed for CTS-MAPF. It extends the conflict-based-search perspective from fixed goals to joint task sequencing and path planning. At the high level, it builds a search forest whose trees correspond to joint task sequences derived from a jTSP solution; at the low level, it computes constrained paths for individual agents. The paper states theoretical guarantees of completeness and optimality in the exact setting, and bounded suboptimality when a bounded parameter is used. It also introduces two evaluation datasets, CTS-MAPF and MG-MAPF, and evaluates the method in simulation and on physical robots.

What is the key technical idea?

CTS-CBS uses a two-level search architecture. The high level maintains multiple search trees, each associated with a candidate joint task sequence, rather than committing to one sequence in advance; new root nodes can be generated when necessary. The low level plans a path for each agent subject to the selected task sequence and the current collision constraints, then returns a joint-path cost to guide the high-level search. When a conflict is detected, the search branches by imposing a constraint on one side or the other of the conflict and replans under the new constraints. This makes task-order decisions and collision resolution interact within the same search process. The implementation uses SIPP for low-level planning, although the paper notes that A*-like methods could also be used.

Detailed section summary

Background

MAPF is a foundational problem in which multiple agents must obtain collision-free paths in a shared workspace, with applications in robotics, warehouses, and autonomous vehicles. Simple methods such as prioritized planning, PUSH AND SWAP, and Hierarchical Cooperative A* are described as easy to deploy but generally non-complete and non-optimal. Conflict-Based Search is presented as a complete and optimal MAPF framework based on high-level conflict constraints and low-level single-agent planning. CTS-MAPF adds collaborative intermediate-task sequencing to that setting.

Limitations of prior work

A CTS-MAPF instance includes agents moving on an undirected workspace graph with vertices, edges, positive edge costs, static obstacles, starts, destinations, and task positions. Each agent may wait or move one step in one of the four cardinal directions, must visit its assigned tasks in the required order, and must then reach its destination. The cost of waiting must be included in the objective. A feasible solution must avoid conflicts and satisfy task assignment, ordering, movement, and arrival constraints while minimizing the total cost.

Technical highlights

The high level organizes a search forest around joint task sequences obtained from a jTSP solver. The low level replans single-agent paths under both sequence requirements and collision constraints, using SIPP in the reported implementation. A necessity check for new root nodes and a tunable suboptimality parameter are key components when bounded suboptimal solutions are preferred. The parameter allows users to exchange solution quality for efficiency, while the paper claims exact behavior in the non-bounded setting.

Results

The reported MG-MAPF adaptations achieve up to a 20-fold improvement in success rate and up to a 100-fold improvement in runtime relative to the baselines. The reported reduction in solution quality is below 10 percent. Physical robots successfully navigate along their designated paths, which the paper uses as evidence of real-world applicability. Conclusion

CTS-CBS integrates collaborative task sequencing with collision-free multi-agent path planning instead of treating sequencing as an independent preliminary step. The paper claims completeness and optimality in its exact setting and bounded suboptimality when a bound parameter is supplied. Its simulation results and physical-robot demonstrations indicate practical benefits, especially for MG-MAPF, in success rate and runtime.

Why it matters to MAPF researchers

The work is important because it treats task assignment, task order, and path conflicts as coupled decisions. Its search structure offers a principled way to reuse conflict-based reasoning when agents have multiple intermediate obligations rather than only one goal. The adjustable quality-efficiency trade-off may be useful in warehouse and multi-robot systems where exact optimality and response time have different operational priorities.
